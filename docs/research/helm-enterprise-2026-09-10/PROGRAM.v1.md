## Delivery order

1. Pin source baselines and replace product-facing OpenCode branding only in the Helm build. Preserve configuration, protocol and upstream provider identities and licensing.
2. Integrate Orca's actual Node runtime through a private, versioned, capability-negotiated boundary. Preserve the actual OpenCode server. Reconcile durable dispatch identities after uncertain outcomes; never resend a prompt merely because its acknowledgement was lost.
3. Extend existing Hades Work rather than creating a competing scheduler: rolling bounded task admission, declared edit reservations, per-task artifact gates, attempt history, durable budgets and clear review/recovery controls.
4. Study current Codex, Claude Code/Cowork and ChatGPT Work. Then inspect pinned OpenWork executable paths in depth, including its MIT/enterprise directory split. Map the complete documented product capabilities to existing, new, unavailable and unverified Hades behavior. Do not claim proprietary internal mechanisms are known.
5. Implement the remaining coding and general-work gaps from that matrix, including connector authority, durable execution, team controls, artifact/review workflows and operating limits. Candidate differentiators are evidence-bound delivery, recovery without duplicate effects, cross-provider teams and native/browser spatial review. Their advantage must be demonstrated, not declared a moat from feature count.
6. Build, exercise representative native workflows, run matched comparisons, fix findings, and admit independent review against the frozen candidate.

## Evidence rules and current external constraints

Receipts record candidate revision, command, timestamps, environment, fixture/oracle hashes, result and limitations. Benchmark expected outputs are separate from prompts. Synthetic fault fixtures verify control logic, not provider intelligence. A product-wide superiority claim requires matched evidence and a stated scope; undocumented proprietary behavior stays unknown.

The requested separate Codex comparison task was attempted on 2026-09-10. Creation returned `MCP tool call requires approval, but approval policy is never`; no separate task exists. This is an open G19 execution gate, not permission to bypass review through another tool. Root network-listener tests are also restricted. Source work, deterministic local tests, primary-source research and permitted native workflows continue independently.

Disk headroom at baseline is below 1 GiB. Build staging must preserve installed/current candidate apps and user data; only identified task-generated regenerable outputs may be cleaned. Local source and mocked/fixture results do not establish installed-app readiness.
